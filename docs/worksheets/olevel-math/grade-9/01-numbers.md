### 📘 Topic Concept

**Order of Operations (BIDMAS / BODMAS)**
Brackets → Indices → Division & Multiplication (left to right) → Addition & Subtraction (left to right).

**Sign rules**

$$(-)\times(-)=(+),\quad (-)\times(+)=(-),\quad (-)\div(-)=(+),\quad (-)\div(+)=(-)$$

**Factors, Multiples, Primes**
- Factor of $n$ → divides $n$ exactly. Multiple of $n$ → any $kn,\; k\in\mathbb{Z}^+$.
- A **prime** has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
- For two integers $a,b$:

$$\text{HCF}(a,b)\times\text{LCM}(a,b)=a\times b$$

**Index Laws**

$$a^m\cdot a^n=a^{m+n},\qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n},\qquad (a^m)^n=a^{mn},\qquad a^0=1\;(a\neq 0),\qquad a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}$$

**Standard Form**
$a\times 10^n$ where $1\le a<10$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.

**Approximation**
- *Decimal places (d.p.)* — count digits **after** the decimal point.
- *Significant figures (s.f.)* — count from the first **non-zero** digit.

**Percentage change**

$$\%\text{ change}=\frac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\times 100\%$$

**Ratio**
To divide $N$ in the ratio $a:b:c$ →  each share $=\dfrac{a}{a+b+c}\times N$.

**Q2.**
**(a)** Successive division by primes: $720=2\cdot 360=2^{2}\cdot 180=2^{3}\cdot 90=2^{4}\cdot 45=2^{4}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot 5$.

$$720=\boxed{2^{4}\times 3^{2}\times 5}$$

**(b)** For $720k$ to be a perfect square every prime exponent must be even. The exponent of $5$ is $1$ (odd), so we need one more factor of $5$:

$$\boxed{k=5}$$

**Q10.**
**(a)** $40=2^{3}\times 5$ — only prime factors $2$ and $5$, so the decimal terminates. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $25$:

$$\dfrac{7}{40}=\dfrac{7\cdot 25}{40\cdot 25}=\dfrac{175}{1000}=\boxed{0.175}$$

**(b)** $12=2^{2}\times 3$. The prime factor $3$ cannot divide any power of $10$ exactly, so no equivalent fraction with denominator $10^{n}$ exists — the decimal cannot terminate.

**(c) Rule.** A fraction $\dfrac{p}{q}$ in lowest terms terminates **if and only if** $q$ has no prime factors other than $2$ and $5$.

*Justification:* a terminating decimal can always be written as $\dfrac{N}{10^{n}}$ for some integer $N$ and $n\ge 0$. Since $10^{n}=2^{n}\times 5^{n}$, any reduced denominator dividing $10^{n}$ can contain only the primes $2$ and $5$. Conversely, if $q=2^{a}\times 5^{b}$, multiplying numerator and denominator by $2^{\max(0,b-a)}\times 5^{\max(0,a-b)}$ produces a denominator $10^{\max(a,b)}$, giving a terminating decimal.
